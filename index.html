Five strikes on Russian data centers, 8–18 October 2026

Between 8 and 18 October 2026, drones hit three Yandex data centers, a Sberbank data center under construction and a VK data center near St Petersburg. This page maps the sites and sets out what operators said, what satellites, network measurements and media coverage show, and which reported disruptions hold true.

    Network reachability

    IODA pings every /24 address block of a network and counts how many answer. The chart shows that count as a share of the 6–7 October median. A fall means address space stopped responding. It does not measure whether a service worked for its users, and it cannot say which building went dark.

    Traffic seen by Cloudflare

    Cloudflare Radar charts for Russia and for the Yandex, Sberbank and VK networks, fixed to 1–19 October 2026. They measure traffic reaching Cloudflare, not reachability, so they complement the IODA chart rather than repeat it. The charts are served live by Cloudflare.

    Media coverage

    How much the press wrote about each operator, from Semantic Visions' svEye media-monitoring service. svEye groups articles from news sources in many languages into events and stories and summarises them automatically. The counts show attention, not facts. The story summaries are machine-generated and have not been checked against sources; where they make claims this investigation could not confirm, that is noted below.

    Method and limits

    Evidence grades

    A
    Operator statement or status page, quoted directly or via a named outlet.
    B
    Third-party measurement: IODA, RIPE, Cloudflare Radar or NetBlocks. My own IODA pull is reproducible; NetBlocks figures are not.
    C
    User-complaint spike (DownDetector, Sboy.rf). Shows that people had problems, not why.
    D
    Media claim without a stated source.

    Locations

    Strike sites are placed on the facility, not on the reported town, using operator addresses, OpenStreetMap footprints and imagery. The data-center layers come from an Overpass query of OpenStreetMap and the PeeringDB facility list for Russia. The Overpass query matched the text “ЦОД” inside unrelated words and pulled in some 200 workwear shops (“спецодежда”), grocery stores and traffic-control centers (ЦОДД). Those were filtered out before publishing: only objects tagged as data centers, or named as one as a whole word, are kept.

    Imagery

    Thermal anomalies come from NASA FIRMS. A FIRMS detection is a satellite pixel of 375 m or 1 km that was hotter than its surroundings during an overpass. It places a fire near a site, not in a particular building, and grass fires look the same as burning buildings. No detection is weak evidence: cloud, timing and small fires all cause misses. Sentinel-2 scenes are 10 m true colour from Copernicus Browser. At 10 m a damaged roof may change tone, but a single building cannot be confirmed as hit. Photographs and videos posted on X are linked, not republished.

    Network data

    IODA reachability counts responsive address blocks for a whole autonomous system. A network that serves several sites can lose one site without a visible drop, and a drop can come from power, routing or deliberate withdrawal as well as physical damage. The Kaluga strike left no visible change in AS200350, which does not show the site was undamaged. Sber's networks are not in the IODA pull; Cloudflare Radar covers them above.

    Open questions

    • Which VK facility at Novosergievka was hit: the 2011 data center or the newer M100 site.
    • The time of the VK strike. The source table gives 03:00 GMT+2, but IODA shows the drop at 02:50–03:00 Moscow time, an hour earlier.
    • The state of the Chervonenkis, Lyapunov and Galushkin supercomputers.
    • Why AS200350 announced 32 new prefixes from 17 October 17:20 UTC.
    • Whether AS47457 (SBERBANK-BALAKOVO-AS) carries traffic for the new Balakovo data center.

    Use of AI

    This investigation was carried out by Kristof Krebs with the assistance of Claude Opus 5.5, a large language model made by Anthropic. Claude was used for parts of the coding (the website, data-processing and chart scripts), data cleaning and analysis (deduplication, filtering the OpenStreetMap layer, the IODA analysis), searching for and summarising sources, and drafting text, including the disruption, recovery and migration tables.
